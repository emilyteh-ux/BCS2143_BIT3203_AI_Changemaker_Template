# Problem Statement
Standard navigation apps optimize for speed, often routing pedestrians through dark, isolated, or high-risk streets at night.

## Target users
University students, night-shift workers, and solo pedestrians walking after dark.

## Evidence
- A two-year Philadelphia street-lighting study found that upgrading streetlights to brighter LED fixtures reduced outdoor street crime at night by 15%. (https://phys.org/news/2025-10-street-safety-effect-crime.html)

- A NYC Mayor's Office survey found 50% of residents feel safe walking in their community during the day, versus just 21% at night. (https://crim.sas.upenn.edu/sites/default/files/streetlights_paper.pdf)

- Governors Highway Safety Association data shows 75% of pedestrian fatalities occur during nighttime hours. (https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/10665095)

- U.S. pedestrian fatality data (2019–2021) shows 21% of fatalities occurred between 6pm and 9pm, the peak low-light exposure window. (https://wifitalents.com/walking-alone-at-night-as-a-woman-statistics/)

## Urgency and social value
Night-time route risk is measurable, not anecdotal lighting, foot traffic, and fatality data all show a clear day/night safety gap. Current navigation tools ignore this gap entirely. A safety-aware routing system directly targets a documented, quantifiable risk rather than a perceived one.

## SDG alignment
Supports UN SDG 11 (Sustainable Cities and Communities) by making urban pedestrian mobility safer after dark.
